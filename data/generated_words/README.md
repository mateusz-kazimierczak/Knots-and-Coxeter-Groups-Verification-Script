# Order-three generated words

This directory preserves the complete output of the two original order-three
word generators and provides normalized files for analysis. Every recorded
word is an exact cube `piece^3`; `piece_length` and `full_length` distinguish
the length of the fundamental piece from the length of the repeated word.

## Contents

```text
generated_words/
    README.md
    order_three_candidates.csv       all outputs, ordered by full length
    summary.csv                      counts by program and length
    raw/
        order_three_bfs.txt          byte-for-byte copy of orderthree.txt
        order_three_randomized_dfs.txt

    through_length_42/
```

## Provenance and counts

The origin program is recorded for every row in the combined CSV.

| Origin program | Full lengths | Candidates |
|---|---|---:|
| Breadth-first search (BFS) | 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36 | 18,855 |
| Randomized depth-first search (DFS) | 24, 36, 42, 48, 54, 60 | 1,219 |

Together the files contain 20,074 output rows and 20,073 distinct words. One
length-24 word was generated by both programs.

The complete counts are in `summary.csv`. The combined
`order_three_candidates.csv` is sorted from shortest to longest and contains:

- `candidate_id`: stable identifier based on the source program and line;
- `origin_program`: BFS or randomized DFS;
- `source_file` and `source_line`: location in the preserved raw data;
- `piece_length`, `repeat_count`, and `full_length`;
- `through_length_42`: whether the full word has length at most 42;
- `also_generated_by_other_program`: whether both searches generated it; and
- `word`: the full order-three word `piece^3`.

## Historical reduction note

According to the project history, the reduction algorithm eliminated most of
what arose below full length 36. Many more length-36 candidates survived the
reduction, but subsequent inspection showed that they were not knots. The
randomized DFS run was the first run in which minimum-length knot examples of
full length 42 were observed.

This paragraph records the history of the search. The source text files do not
contain per-candidate reduction or knot-classification results, so the CSV does
not assign those labels to individual rows. Its rows should be read as
generated candidates, not as assertions that every word represents a knot.
